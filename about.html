The Competition Current Format

The English Premier League consists of 20 teams that compete in a round-robin format, playing each other twice (home and away) for a total of 38 matches per team. The team with the most points at the end of the season is crowned the champion. The bottom three teams are relegated to the EFL Championship, while the top four teams qualify for the UEFA Champions League.

Prominent Clubs

  • Liverpool (20 titles)
  • Manchester United (20 titles)
  • Arsenal (13 titles)
  • Chelsea (6 titles)
  • Manchester City (8 titles)

Liverpool

Liverpool stands as the most successful English club of the pre-Premier League era, having won 18 league titles, compared to just two during the Premier League era. Although the team was consistently a strong title contender, they fell short on numerous occasions until German manager Jürgen Klopp arrived in 2015 and led them to the league title in 2020, ending a thirty-year drought. The club boasts one of the most loyal fanbases in the sport, and facing them at their home ground, Anfield, is invariably one of the toughest challenges for any opposing team.

Manchester United

Manchester United won their first league title in the 1992/1993 season, followed by 12 more, making them the most successful club of that era; they secured all these titles under the leadership of their legendary manager, Sir Alex Ferguson, who retired in 2013. After the manager's retirement, conditions deteriorated, and the club failed to win a single title for 13 consecutive years.

Arsenal

Arsenal is one of the clubs that has fielded squads highly competitive for the league title. Among the most notable was the team led by their legendary manager Arsène Wenger, who managed the club for 22 years, specifically the "Invincibles" squad of the 2003/2004 season, which won the league without losing a single one of their 38 matches. Key players from this generation included Thierry Henry, Patrick Vieira, and Dennis Bergkamp; notably, they were the last Arsenal squad to win the league title, a feat the club has not repeated in the 22 years since.

Chelsea

Chelsea Football Club is one of those clubs that truly rose to prominence during the Premier League era, specifically following the 2003 takeover by Russian billionaire Roman Abramovich. This acquisition ushered in a period of heavy investment and the signing of numerous players; however, the club's most significant move was the appointment of manager José Mourinho. He led the team to its first league title in 50 years, paving the way for the club to secure four additional league titles thereafter.

Manchester City

Manchester City had a rough start in the Premier League era, including getting relegated. Things changed after the 2008 Emirati takeover, and they won the league in 2012 with Agüero's unforgettable last-minute goal at the last minute of the last game of the season. With their legendary manager Pep Guardiola, they reached 100 points in 2018, which is the most points by a team in a single season. They later won four league titles in a row from 2021 to 2024, something no English top-flight club had done before.
